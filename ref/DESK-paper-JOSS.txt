Abstract

DESK is an open-source framework for discrete-event simulation that integrates replication management, experimental design, and result aggregation as first-class components of the simulation workflow. While many simulation tools focus on model construction and execution, DESK addresses the recurring methodological challenges faced by researchers and students when designing simulation experiments, managing replications, and interpreting results across multiple scenarios. The framework provides a modular architecture that separates model logic from experimental configuration, enabling reproducible factorial and scenario-based analyses. DESK is designed to support both research and teaching applications, emphasizing transparency, reproducibility, and extensibility.

In practice, discrete-event simulation studies require systematic management of replications, scenario definitions, and experimental designs. These elements are often implemented in an ad hoc manner, leading to limited reproducibility and increased implementation effort. While existing simulation libraries provide robust mechanisms for model execution, support for experimental design and replication management is typically left to the user. DESK addresses this gap by explicitly structuring simulation experiments as configurable, repeatable, and analyzable objects.
